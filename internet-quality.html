Internet Quality Thresholds

A testbed that finds how much packet delay it takes to stall YouTube Live.

  • C
  • Linux Netfilter / NFQUEUE
  • Python
  • Docker
  • Selenium via NetGent
  • Snakemake
Read the paper (PDF) ↗My commits in NetGent ↗Full research write-up →

No hosted demo: the testbed needs three machines. Figures are from the paper.

Problem
Internet quality scores (M-Lab IQB, Cloudflare AIM, IETF QoO) need thresholds for when latency makes an app unusable. Today those come from expert opinion.
What I owned
First author, with Prof. Ranysha Ware. I built the testbed, wrote the C program that delays packets through NFQUEUE, and automated the sweeps and analysis. In NetGent I wrote the YouTube Live and Twitch workflows and changed the playback logger.
Result
No added delay: 0% rebuffering. 25 ms on one packet in five: median 31.9% rebuffering over four runs. Accepted as a poster paper at ACM CoNEXT 2026.

Key decision

Pin the stream to 1080p. Sample every 0.1 s.

Why
With resolution fixed, delay can only show up as buffering. Logging every 0.1 s instead of every 2 s catches stalls shorter than a second.
Trade-off
It hides how adaptive bitrate would lower quality to avoid stalls. The sweep covers 5 delays × 4 frequencies to limit load on YouTube, so small effects can be missed.
